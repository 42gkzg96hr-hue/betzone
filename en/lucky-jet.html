1. What is Lucky Jet?

Lucky Jet is a crash game built by 1win's own studio — so it's exclusive to 1win. The idea is dead simple: a character (Lucky Joe) blasts off with a jetpack and a multiplier rises (x1.2… x1.8… x3…). You can cash out at any moment to multiply your bet by the current value. Wait too long and Lucky Joe flies off, the round "crashes", and you lose your bet.

It's a fast game of chance, not a skill game. The only decision you control is when you cash out. Everything else is random.

2. How to play Lucky Jet

  1. Open a 1win account and fund it (find the game under "1win Games" / crash).
  2. Choose your bet (often from $0.10 equivalent).
  3. Optional: set an auto cash-out (e.g. x1.5).
  4. Start the round — the multiplier climbs.
  5. Cash out before the crash. Winnings = bet × multiplier at cash-out.

Lucky Jet also allows two parallel bets — cash one out early to secure it and let the other ride.

3. The only honest strategy

Let's be clear: no strategy makes Lucky Jet profitable long-term. The game has a built-in house edge. Every "prediction software", "signal" and "hack" you see is a scam — nobody can predict the crash; it's random and verifiable.

The only approach that works is cash-out discipline:

  • Set your target before the round (e.g. cash out at x1.5) and stick to it.
  • Small multipliers cashed out often beat big multipliers never reached. Greed crashes profits, not bad luck.
  • Use auto cash-out so emotion doesn't take over.

4. Concrete tips

  • Fixed budget: decide what you can lose BEFORE playing, and stop there.
  • Small bets: 50 small bets beat one big bet — you last longer and stay in control.
  • Two bets: secure the first early (x1.3–x1.5), let the second aim higher.
  • Never chase losses — raising the bet to "win it back" is trap #1.
  • Set a deposit limit in the app.

5. RTP & provably fair

Lucky Jet has a ~97% RTP — a long-run average across millions of rounds, not a promise for your session. It's provably fair: a cryptographic system lets you verify a result wasn't rigged after your bet. Great for transparency — but "fair" doesn't mean "favourable"; the house edge remains.
