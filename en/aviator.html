Aviator Game: How to Play (and the truth about predictors)

Aviator is the most popular crash game right now. Here are the rules, the cash-out, the RTP, the auto mode — and above all the honest truth about the "predictors" and "hacks" sold everywhere. The goal: help you play informed.

1. What is the Aviator game?

Aviator (by Spribe) is a crash game: a little plane takes off, a multiplier rises (x1.1… x2… x5…), and it can "fly away" at any moment. Your goal is to cash out before it leaves. Cash out at x2 and you double your bet; if the plane flies off first, you lose the bet. Simple, fast and thrilling — which is why it's so popular worldwide.

2. Rules & how to play

  1. Place your bet before take-off (you can place two bets at once).
  2. The plane takes off and the multiplier climbs.
  3. Hit Cash Out to collect at the displayed multiplier.
  4. If the plane flies away before you cash out, the bet is lost.

Auto mode

Aviator has an auto cash-out: set a value (e.g. x1.8) and the game collects automatically when it's hit. Great for avoiding emotional decisions. There's also auto-bet (a fixed bet each round).

3. The truth about predictors & hacks

Let's be blunt: there is no way to predict when the plane will fly away. The result is generated randomly (RNG) and is provably fair. So:

  • "Aviator predictor" apps, hacks and paid "signals" are scams. They cannot work, technically.
  • "The plane is due for a big multiplier" is a gambler's fallacy — every round is independent.
  • The martingale (double after each loss) eventually blows up your bankroll; it does not beat the house edge.

The only things you actually control are your bet size and your cash-out timing. Everything else is chance.

4. Honest tips

  • Budget first: set an amount you can afford to lose and stop there.
  • Low, steady auto cash-out: aiming for x1.4–x1.8 on auto smooths risk better than chasing x10.
  • Two bets: cash out the first one early to secure it, let the second run.
  • Withdraw your wins regularly instead of re-betting everything.
  • Never chase losses — raising your bet after a loss is the classic trap.
  • Turn on a deposit limit in the app.

5. RTP & fairness

Aviator has a ~97% RTP: across millions of rounds, ~97 returned for every 100 wagered. The remaining 3% is the house edge — which is why no player wins long-term. The game is provably fair (you can verify the result wasn't manipulated), but "fair" doesn't mean "in your favour": the house edge is always there.

FAQ — Aviator

How to win at Aviator every time?

You can't. It's a game of chance with a house edge. You can only manage your bet and cash-out with discipline.

Do Aviator predictors work?

No. The outcome is random and provably fair — no tool can predict it. They are scams.

What auto cash-out should I use?

Many cautious players use x1.4–x1.8. What matters is setting your value in advance and sticking to it.
