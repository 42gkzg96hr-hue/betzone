Betting in Burkina Faso: the state runs its own book, and everyone else is in a grey zone

The LONAB launched its own online sportsbook in December 2023 and had more than 45,000 registered players within days. Everything outside it sits in a legal space nobody has defined. Here is what the 2008 law actually says, which payment rails are licensed, and the 15% nobody mentions.

One law, a ministry, and no regulator

Gambling here runs on law 027-2008/AN of 8 May 2008, promulgated by decree in June of the same year. Article 2 makes any speculative game of chance subject to prior authorisation from the Ministry of Finance; article 3 names sports betting explicitly among them.

There is no independent gambling authority. Authorisation is ministerial, discretionary and revocable, granted jointly with the security ministry. You will see comparison sites refer to a Commission Nationale des Jeux de Hasard or a Burkinabe gaming control board. We could not find either in any official Burkinabe source and we believe they do not exist. Do not take a licence claim referencing them seriously.

The state operator is the LONAB, the Loterie Nationale Burkinabè, which runs Faso Pari, PMU B, Faso Cash and the rest. On 26 December 2023 it launched online sports betting at its own domain, at a ceremony in Ouagadougou chaired by the finance minister. By the following day it reported 45,097 registered users and more than 40,000 active bettors, with deposits and withdrawals by mobile money or by voucher bought in a LONAB agency.

At that launch the LONAB said plainly that certain illegal operators were offering online betting in the country, and positioned its own product as the lawful alternative.

Where foreign operators actually stand

We are going to be straight with you, because the sites that claim certainty here are guessing.

Two instruments are cited as granting the LONAB a monopoly over sports betting: a 2010 decree and a 1996 law. Neither could be located in a primary source. Meanwhile no licensing route exists for a foreign online operator — not a closed one, not a difficult one, simply none. The honest description is that offering online betting from abroad is neither authorised nor prohibited by any text we could read, and that it depends on a discretionary ministerial authorisation that is not published.

Penalties for running an unauthorised operation are reported as two months to two years imprisonment and a fine of 250,000 to 1,000,000 FCFA, with no offence identified for the player — but that too comes from a secondary source we could not confirm against the penal code.

We searched specifically for any measure, decree or ban concerning sports betting adopted under the transition government between 2024 and 2026. We found none.

The 15% on your winnings that nobody advertises

Article 225 of the Burkinabè General Tax Code sets a 15% final withholding levy on the winnings of bettors and other games of chance. The rate was introduced by law 029-2022/ALT of 24 December 2022 and applies from 1 January 2023.

Article 348 imposes a gaming tax on all games except those under LONAB monopoly, and LONAB products instead carry a 5% stamp duty under article 517. The 2025 budget proposed harmonising everything at 5% by amending article 348 and repealing 517. Whether that harmonisation survived into the promulgated finance law of 23 December 2024, we could not establish — one source says it passed, another says it was withdrawn.

Conflicting sources, stated openly. One widely copied gambling guide claims lottery and sports betting winnings are untaxed in Burkina Faso. The tax code says 15%. We trust the tax code, but we could not confirm who operates the withholding, nor how it applies to winnings paid by an operator with no presence in the country. Check with the DGI before assuming either way.

Mobile money: who is actually licensed

The BCEAO register of electronic money institutions lists, for Burkina Faso: Orange Money, Moov Money through UBA Burkina and Moov Africa, Coris Money through Coris Bank, and Wave through UBA Burkina and Orabank, alongside Wizall, Telecel and BMoney.

Wave is genuinely established here, not a marginal player: it has operated since 2021, opened its 24th premium branch in Kaya in October 2026, launched a wallet-to-bank service with Orabank in July 2026, and joined the BCEAO instant payment platform on 30 September 2026.

Worth correcting, because an English-language blog keeps repeating it: MTN MoMo does not exist in Burkina Faso. MTN is not a mobile operator in the country at all — the three networks are Orange, Moov Africa and Telecel Faso.

We are not publishing transfer fees, ceilings or minimum amounts. No official tariff schedule from Orange Money, Moov Money, Coris Money or Wave Burkina was readable, and the figures floating around comparison sites have no source behind them.

The currency is the CFA franc (XOF), pegged at 655.957 to the euro, which put it around 585 FCFA to the US dollar in October 2026.

Fasofoot, and the fall of the most decorated club in the country

The top flight is Fasofoot Ligue 1, with the Coupe du Faso as the national cup. Rahimo FC were champions in 2025-26, finishing on 69 points with a goal difference of plus 32, fifteen clear of USFA in second. The season ran from 20 September 2025 to 14 June 2026.

The story of the season was at the other end. Étoile Filante de Ouagadougou — thirteen league titles and a record fifteen Coupes du Faso, the most decorated club in Burkina Faso — were relegated to Ligue 2 on 23 May 2026. The historic derby against ASFA Yennenga, their equal on thirteen titles, therefore is not on the Ligue 1 calendar.

Our sources disagree on how many league titles Rahimo now hold, so we are not giving you a number.

The Stallions, the World Cup that got away, and a disputed one

Burkina Faso is not at the 2026 World Cup. The Stallions finished fifth among the African runners-up and missed the play-offs on 14 October 2025: a 3-1 win over Ethiopia was not enough once DR Congo beat Sudan 1-0, leaving Burkina behind Nigeria and the Congolese on goal difference.

There is a sting in the tail. In October 2026 an investigation by Le Monde and the New York Times reported that FIFA lawyers had concluded goalkeeper Lionel Mpasi was ineligible for DR Congo, his change of association from France never having been completed, and that Burkina Faso rather than DR Congo should have taken the African play-off place. Article 19 of the disciplinary code provided for a forfeit; it was not applied. No sporting sanction followed, the Nigerian federation appeal was ruled inadmissible on procedural grounds, and DR Congo went on to qualify by beating Jamaica 1-0 after extra time on 31 March 2026. Nothing has been reopened.

In the AFCON 2027 qualifiers Burkina Faso are in Group F with Benin, Mauritania and the Central African Republic, sitting second on 4 points after a 1-1 draw with Benin and a 1-0 win away to the CAR. The top two in each group go through. Matchday three, Burkina Faso against Mauritania, falls in the window of 11 to 17 November 2026.

Connectivity, and a risk worth naming

Two numbers circulate and they measure different things. The telecoms regulator counted 19.4 million mobile internet subscriptions at 31 March 2026, a 78.61% penetration rate — but those are SIM subscriptions, not people. DataReportal put actual internet users at 5.75 million, around 24% of the population. Do not read the first figure as meaning three quarters of Burkinabè are online.

On access: no internet shutdown has been documented in 2025 or 2026. But the precedent is real. Mobile internet was cut for 96 hours from 20 November 2021 during protests, officially acknowledged by the communications ministry, with further disruptions around the January 2022 coup. The legal power to restrict access exists and has been used. Starlink remains unauthorised in the country, with negotiations reported as progressing in July 2026 and no timetable.

If you bet here, assume connectivity is good until it suddenly is not, and do not leave a balance somewhere you might not be able to reach for a few days.

What we could not verify

  • The decree or law founding the LONAB monopoly. Cited second-hand only.
  • Any licence regime for foreign online operators. None exists, so no site can hold a Burkinabè one.
  • The criminal penalties for unauthorised operation, in the penal code itself.
  • Whether the 5% harmonisation made it into the 2025 finance law.
  • Fees, ceilings and minimum amounts on any mobile money rail.
  • The article fixing the minimum age. The LONAB site carries an 18+ notice and its betting product requires 18, but we could not read the statutory provision.

Questions Burkinabè bettors actually ask

Is it illegal for me to bet on a foreign site?

No offence for the player was identified in any source we could read. The legal exposure described in the 2008 law and the penal code concerns whoever operates without authorisation, not whoever places a bet.

Is the LONAB the only legal option?

It is the only operator we can confirm is authorised. Whether that amounts to a formal monopoly rests on texts we could not locate.

Will my winnings be taxed?

The tax code sets a 15% final levy on gambling winnings. How it is collected, and whether it reaches winnings paid from abroad, is not documented. Ask the DGI.

Which mobile money service should I use?

Any of the licensed ones, but use the same one for deposits and withdrawals, and make sure the account is in your own name. Name mismatches are the single most common cause of a blocked payout.
