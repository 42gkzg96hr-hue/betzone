Betting in The Gambia: the 50% tax on winnings, and everything it changes

Since January 2026 the Gambian state takes half of every betting win, deducted before the money reaches you. That one figure affects your bankroll more than any bonus offer, and almost no betting site mentions it. Here is what the budget actually says, what the law covers, and what nobody can verify.

Half of your winnings go to the Gambia Revenue Authority

The 2026 Budget Speech, presented to the National Assembly on 5 December 2025 by the Minister of Finance, raised the tax on gambling winnings to 50%. The climb is recent and steep: 30% until 2024, 40% through 2025, 50% from 2026.

Two details decide what this costs you in practice.

First, the tax applies to winnings excluding the amount staked. Put D500 on a selection at odds of 3.00 and the ticket returns D1,500. The taxable figure is the D1,000 of profit, not the D1,500. You walk away with your D500 stake plus D500, so D1,000 in hand instead of D1,500.

Second, you never hand the money over yourself. The operator withholds at source and files a monthly Pool Betting Tax Return with the Gambia Revenue Authority, form RET 18, due fifteen days after the end of each month. It lists every winning ticket, the amount won, the tax withheld and the net paid out. The deduction happens before the payout reaches your wallet.

The Treasury expects the measure to raise D72.7 million in 2026, and 5% of pool betting receipts, roughly D5 million, is earmarked for the Ministry of Youth and Sports.

An honest caveat. We could not locate the amending legislation that fixes the rate at 50%. The figure rests on the Budget Speech, an official document but not a statute. If you are planning around a large payout, confirm the current rate with the GRA directly rather than trusting any website, this one included.

There is no gambling regulator in The Gambia

This surprises people, so it is worth stating plainly: the country has no gambling board, no gaming authority and no online licensing regime. Responsibility is split three ways. The Gambia Tourism Board licenses casinos under section 32(1) of the Tourism Board Act 2011. The Gambia Revenue Authority administers the Betting and Gaming Act as a revenue law. The Financial Intelligence Unit is meant to supervise casinos for money laundering, though the GIABA mutual evaluation published in 2022 found it had never actually done so.

Betting has not always been permitted here. The Office of the President banned lotteries, casinos and all forms of gambling on 2 March 2015. The ban was lifted on 26 May 2017 after the change of government and betting shops reopened. Nothing comparable has happened since, and no gambling bill appeared on the National Assembly bill tracker as of September 2026.

Online betting sits in a genuine grey area rather than an illegal one. The Criminal Offences Act 2025, assented on 28 March 2025, makes keeping a common gaming house or common betting house an offence but exempts premises licensed under the Pool Betting (Licensing) Act. Nothing in it creates or forbids an online licence, because no online regime exists at all. Local reporting by Foroyaa in December 2023 described betting firms taking wagers through apps and street agents with the existing law going unenforced.

What this means for you is concrete: no Gambian authority is holding your operator to anything. If a withdrawal is refused there is no regulator to complain to. That is not a reason to panic, but it is a reason to keep your own records of every transaction.

Only two mobile money services are actually licensed

The Central Bank of The Gambia licenses exactly two electronic money issuers: Afrimoney, operated by Africell, and QMoney, operated by Qcell. Everything else you see advertised is a payment service provider rather than a mobile money operator, including Wave, which appears on the CBG register under the name Wave Transfer. The distinction is not academic: the licence type decides who the central bank holds responsible when a transfer goes missing.

The wider picture is unusually good. Formal financial inclusion reached 82% in 2025, up from 19% in 2019 according to the FinScope survey, one of the fastest shifts recorded anywhere, and driven almost entirely by mobile money. GamSwitch handles interbank and card switching nationally.

We are deliberately not publishing minimum deposits, withdrawal limits or transfer fees. Every figure we found traced back to commercial comparison sites with no primary source behind it. Check the current numbers inside your own Afrimoney or QMoney app before planning a deposit.

The domestic league is small, and that cuts both ways

The GFF League First Division has run since 1965 and fields 16 clubs. Medina United took the 2025-26 title, their first. Real de Banjul won the season before, their fifteenth. Wallidan hold the record with sixteen, though their last came in 2008.

Matches are played at Box Bar Stadium in Banjul, capacity around 3,000, at Serrekunda East Mini Stadium, around 5,000, and at Independence Stadium in Bakau, around 30,000. Crowds are modest, often a few dozen for an ordinary league fixture.

For a bettor this matters in a specific way. Thin public data and low bookmaker attention mean prices on Gambian league matches are set with less information than, say, a Premier League fixture. That is often sold as an edge. It is at least as often a trap: you do not have more information than the bookmaker, team news is scarce, and a single late withdrawal can swing a result. Treat these markets with more caution than the big European leagues, not less.

The historic rivalry is Real de Banjul against Wallidan, the two most decorated clubs in the country. Be careful with fixtures though: our sources disagree on whether Wallidan are currently in the First Division or the Second, so do not assume the derby is on this season calendar.

The Scorpions in the AFCON 2027 qualifiers

The Gambia are coached by Johnny McKinstry, captained by Omar Colley, and sat 116th in the FIFA ranking as of 20 July 2026. The team has never reached a World Cup in fourteen attempts and did not qualify for 2026, nor for AFCON 2025. Its two AFCON appearances came in 2021, a quarter-final lost to hosts Cameroon, and 2023.

They sit in Group C of the AFCON 2027 qualifiers and have started well, taking 6 points from two matchdays, level on points with Cote d Ivoire at the top. The opener was a 2-1 win over Somalia settled by Muhammed Badamosi after 72 minutes. The next fixture is away to Cote d Ivoire on 11 November 2026.

The squad is built around players earning abroad: Yankuba Minteh at Brighton, Alieu Fadera at Cagliari, Adama Bojang at Reims, Ebou Adams at Derby County. Musa Barrow remains the all-time top scorer with 14 goals.

What we could not verify

Most betting guides never publish a section like this. We do, because an admitted gap is worth more than a figure you cannot check.

  • The legal minimum age for betting in The Gambia. No reliable source found. Assume 18+ and expect the operator to enforce its own rule.
  • Any minimum deposit or withdrawal amount, in dalasi or otherwise.
  • Whether cryptocurrency is used or regulated for betting here. The central bank publishes nothing on it.
  • The operator-side tax rate, licence fees, and whether any national self-exclusion scheme exists. No data at all.
  • Any Gambian online betting licence. There is no register, no authority and no regime, so a site claiming to hold one is misleading you.

Questions Gambian bettors actually ask

Is online betting legal in The Gambia?

Licensed land-based pool betting is legal and taxed. Online betting is neither authorised nor prohibited: no law creates a licence for it and none bans it. You are not committing an offence by placing a bet. The operator simply is not regulated here.

Do I have to declare my winnings?

If the operator withholds correctly, the tax is taken before you are paid. We could not confirm how this works for winnings from an operator with no Gambian presence. Ask the GRA.

Is 50% really the highest rate in Africa?

Trade press has described it that way. We have not independently compared every African jurisdiction, so we are not repeating the claim as a fact.

Which payment method should I use?

Whichever one you can withdraw to. The most common cause of a blocked payout, anywhere, is a mismatch between the name on the betting account and the name on the mobile money account.
